Two changes to scope and dates.

Both make the term easier, not harder. Read the first one before you plan your revision, and the second before you plan your weekends.

01The midterm is smaller

Session 10 · Wednesday 14 October 2026 · 20 multiple-choice questions, four options each

Was

Sessions 1–9

Up to and including residual diagnostics.

→

Now

Sessions 1–8

Up to and including benchmark forecasting methods and fitted values.

What moved Session 9, residual diagnostics, is no longer on the midterm. It is examined in the final instead, together with train/test accuracy (Session 13) and cross-validation (Session 14). Those three sessions are one idea — how you tell whether a model deserves to be believed — and they are now examined together rather than split across two exams.
What this does not mean Session 9 still matters. Residual diagnostics is the most-used skill in the second half of the course, and Group Assignment 2 depends on it. It is deferred, not dropped.
What you gain Session 9 becomes a revision session. Monday 5 October: the first half teaches diagnostics, the second half is a full revision sweep of Sessions 1–8, driven by your questions. Bring questions rather than notes.

Why after the exam Revising for 20 multiple-choice questions and building a decomposition from scratch are different activities. Making them compete for the same fortnight helped nobody, so the deadline sits after the midterm. Start reading the brief in the week it is released, though — choosing your series is half the work.

04How you get feedback

Homework is graded on completion and genuine effort, not on correctness

1 · The day after

A cohort-wide common-errors note goes up on Blackboard, built from what the whole class actually got wrong.

2 · Next session

The first ten minutes of the first session after the deadline review it live.

3 · Rotating sample

Individual written comments go to a different slice of the class each week, so everyone is commented on individually several times over the term.

No published solutions Worked solutions are not posted. Most exercises are self-verifying by design: you compute something by hand and check it against R's own answer with isTRUE(all.equal(x, y)). That check is the feedback, and it arrives while you are still working rather than a week later.
One exception, in one week only Session 09's homework is reviewed at Session 11, not at the next session — because the next session is the midterm. That week splits in two:
  • Monday 12 October — the written common-errors note goes up on Blackboard, two days before the exam, so you can use it while revising.
  • Monday 19 October — the live review happens at the start of Session 11.

05What to plan for

  • About 1.5 hours per session outside class, covering the pre-session reading and the homework. The decomposition block (Sessions 5–7) and the weeks around each group assignment run heavier than that.
  • Read the assigned fpp3 sections before class, not after. Every session README names them. Skim the examples; do not try to solve them.
  • Homework must be your own individual attempt. The policy on generative AI is strict: AI-generated code or results are not allowed. See SYLLABUS.md.
