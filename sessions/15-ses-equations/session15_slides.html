    Session 15 · Exponential smoothing

    Simple exponential smoothing learns from each forecast error.

    Simple exponential smoothing (SES) estimates the current level of a series that has no persistent trend or seasonality. When an observation arrives, its difference from the earlier forecast is the forecast error, or surprise. SES uses a fraction of that error to revise the level, which then becomes the next forecast.

    Session 15 · Updating the SES level

    Predict first. Update after observing.

    et = yt − ℓt−1 ℓt = ℓt−1 + α et
    • At time t, yt is the observation and ℓt−1 is the previous level. Their difference, et, is the forecast error.
    • The smoothing weight α (alpha), between 0 and 1, determines how much of that error moves the estimate to its new level, ℓt.

    Session 15 · Updating the SES level

    The new level blends the observation with the old level.

    ℓt−1 + α(yt − ℓt−1) = αyt + (1 − α)ℓt−1
    • The observation yt receives weight α (alpha), while the previous level ℓt−1 receives 1 − α. Because these weights sum to one, the result is a weighted average.
    • A larger smoothing weight α therefore moves the level further toward the new observation. A smaller weight retains more of the previous estimate.

    Session 15 · SES observation weights

    Repeated updates give older observations less weight.

    ℓ1 = αy1 + (1−α)ℓ0 ℓ2 = αy2 + α(1−α)y1 + (1−α)²ℓ0 ℓT = ∑j=0T−1 α(1−α)jyT−j + (1−α)Tℓ0

    Here ℓ denotes the estimated level, y an observation, and α the smoothing weight. After T observations, yT−j is j steps old, so its weight includes j factors of 1 − α. The initial level ℓ0 retains the remaining weight, (1−α)T.

    Session 15 · SES observation weights

    Observation weights and the initial-level weight sum to one.

    ST = α + αr + ... + αrT−1, r = 1−α ST − rST = α(1−rT) ST = 1−rT for α > 0 Observation weights + initial-level weight = 1
    • ST adds the observation weights after T updates, with smoothing weight α. At α = 0.5, the latest of two observations receives weight 0.5 and the earlier one 0.25, leaving 0.25 for the initial level ℓ0.
    • When α = 0, every forecast equals that initial level. This constant becomes the sample mean if fitting chooses it to minimize SSE, the sum of squared errors.

    Session 15 · SES observation weights

    A smaller alpha retains more of the older information.

    • The smoothing weight α (alpha) determines how quickly observation weights decay. At α = 0.1, about a third of the total weight still rests on the initial level after ten observations.
    • At α = 1, the forecast equals the latest observation: the naive method. Smaller values adapt more slowly because earlier observations retain weight, although their weights remain unequal.

    Session 15 · Fitting and forecasting SES

    SES gives every future period the same point forecast.

    Prediction at time t: ŷt|t−1 = ℓt−1 Future forecast: ŷT+h|T = ℓT
    • The notation ŷt|t−1 means a forecast for time t using information through t−1. It equals the previous level ℓt−1. After the last observation T, every horizon h uses the final level ℓT.
    • SES expects future errors to average zero, which explains the flat path. It suits a changing level without persistent trend or seasonality, while accumulating future errors can widen its prediction intervals.

    Session 15 · Fitting and forecasting SES

    This year's prediction uses last year's level.

    The annual Algerian exports dataset measures exports as a percentage of GDP. These rounded values illustrate its first observation.

    Smoothing weight α = 0.84; initial level ℓ0 = 39.54 First observation y1 = 39.04; prior prediction = 39.54 Forecast error e1 = 39.04 − 39.54 = −0.50 Updated level = 0.84 × 39.04 + 0.16 × 39.54 = 39.12

    The fitted value for year 1 is 39.54 because it precedes that observation. The revised level, 39.12, forecasts year 2.

    Session 15 · Fitting and forecasting SES

    Fitting finds the smoothing weight and starting level.

    • For each candidate α (the smoothing weight) and initial level, run the updates on the training data. The sum of squared one-step errors, SSE, measures how closely that pair fits.
    • Choose the pair with the lowest training SSE, then evaluate forecasts on observations held out of fitting. The evaluation horizon is how far ahead the business decision requires a forecast.

    Session 15 · Algerian exports dataset

    The R specification fits SES to the exports dataset.

    Here algeria_economy contains the annual Algerian exports dataset, with Exports measured as a percentage of GDP.

    fit <- algeria_economy |> model(ETS(Exports ~ error("A") + trend("N") + season("N"))) tidy(fit) augment(fit) forecast(fit, h = 5)
    • The letters specify additive errors (A), no trend (N) and no seasonality (N), giving the SES model.
    • tidy() reports the smoothing weight and starting level. augment() returns fitted values, and forecast() projects five years ahead with prediction intervals.
