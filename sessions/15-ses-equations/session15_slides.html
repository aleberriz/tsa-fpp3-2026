    Session 15 · The block

    A forecast learns from a surprise.

    Simple exponential smoothing updates an estimate of the current level. We will connect the update, its weights and the forecasts it produces.

    Session 15 · The update

    Predict first. Update after observing.

    et = yt − ℓt−1 ℓt = ℓt−1 + α et

    Session 15 · The update

    The correction is a weighted average.

    ℓt−1 + α(yt − ℓt−1) = αyt + (1 − α)ℓt−1
    • Alpha controls how much of this surprise changes the level.
    • Large alpha responds quickly; small alpha retains more of the previous estimate.
    Read the finite weighted-average form

    Session 15 · The weights

    Unfold the update to see its memory.

    ℓ1 = αy1 + (1−α)ℓ0 ℓ2 = αy2 + α(1−α)y1 + (1−α)²ℓ0 ℓT = ∑j=0T−1 α(1−α)jyT−j + (1−α)Tℓ0

    Each step back multiplies an observation's weight by 1 − alpha.

    Session 15 · The weights

    The initial level carries the remaining weight.

    ST = α + αr + ... + αrT−1, r = 1−α ST − rST = α(1−rT) ST = 1−rT for α > 0 Observation weights + initial-level weight = 1
    • With alpha = 0.5 and two observations: 0.5 + 0.25 + 0.25 = 1.
    • At alpha = 0 the level stays at its initial value. It is the sample mean only if that constant is fitted by SSE.

    Session 15 · The weights

    Alpha changes how far the memory reaches.

    • All panels use the same vertical scale. The initial-level weight is not shown.
    • At alpha = 1 the forecast is naive. Small alpha means slow adaptation, not automatically the sample mean.

    Session 15 · Fit and forecast

    A level-only model forecasts a flat path.

    Prediction at time t: ŷt|t−1 = ℓt−1 Future forecast: ŷT+h|T = ℓT
    • Suitable when a changing level is plausible, without persistent trend or seasonality.
    • Uncertainty can grow with the horizon even when the point forecast is flat.
